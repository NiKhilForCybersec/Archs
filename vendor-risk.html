Vendor Risk Management

Your security is only as strong as your weakest vendor. Third-party risk management (TPRM) assesses, monitors, and mitigates risks from suppliers, SaaS providers, and business partners who have access to your data or systems.

Vendor Risk Lifecycle

Vendor Risk Tiering

Tier Criteria Assessment Review Frequency
Critical Access to sensitive data, business critical, high breach impact Full assessment, SOC 2 Type II, pen test report, on-site audit Annual + continuous monitoring
High Access to confidential data, important but not critical Full questionnaire, SOC 2, ISO 27001 cert Annual
Medium Limited data access, internal use tools Standard questionnaire, self-attestation Every 2 years
Low No data access, commodity services Basic due diligence, website review As needed

4. NETWORK SECURITY
   □ Firewall and segmentation?
   □ Intrusion detection/prevention?

5. BUSINESS CONTINUITY
   □ Disaster recovery plan?
   □ Backup and recovery testing?
   □ RTO/RPO commitments?

6. COMPLIANCE & CERTIFICATIONS
   □ SOC 2 Type II (request report)

7. SUBPROCESSORS
   □ List of subprocessors?
   □ How are they assessed?
   □ Notification of changes?

Contract Security Requirements

Essential Contract Clauses

Data Processing Agreement (DPA): Required for GDPR. Defines how vendor handles personal data.

Security Requirements: Minimum security controls, encryption standards, access controls.

Breach Notification: Notify within 24-72 hours. Provide root cause and remediation.

Right to Audit: Ability to audit vendor security or receive audit reports.

Subprocessor Approval: Notification and approval rights for new subprocessors.

Data Return/Deletion: Upon termination, return or certify deletion of data.

Cyber Insurance: Minimum coverage requirements.

Vendor Risk Best Practices

Vendor Risk Implementation Guidelines

1. Know Your Vendors: Maintain complete inventory. Shadow IT means unknown vendors with unknown risk.

2. Risk-Based Approach: Focus resources on critical/high vendors. Not all vendors need full assessment.

3. Leverage Certifications: SOC 2 Type II reduces questionnaire burden. Trust but verify.

4. Continuous Monitoring: Annual assessments miss mid-year breaches. Use rating services for real-time visibility.

5. Involve Legal: Contract clauses are your protection. Security requirements must be enforceable.

6. Plan for Vendor Breach: When (not if) a vendor is breached, know your response plan.
