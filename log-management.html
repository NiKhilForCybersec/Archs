Log Management

Effective log management is the foundation of security operations. Centralized collection, proper retention, and fast search capabilities enable threat detection, incident response, and compliance. Without comprehensive logging, you're flying blind.

Log Retention Requirements

PCI-DSS

1 year retention, 3 months immediately accessible. Daily log reviews required. All access to cardholder data environments.

HIPAA

6 years retention. Access to PHI, system activity, security incidents. Audit controls required.

SOX

7 years retention for financial systems. Change management, access logs for systems affecting financial reporting.

GDPR

No specific retention, but must justify. Balance security needs vs. data minimization. Document retention rationale.

Practical Retention Strategy

Hot (0-30 days): Full detail, fast search. Active investigations, real-time detection.

Warm (30-90 days): Full detail, slower search. Incident response, trend analysis.

Cold (90 days - 1 year): Compressed, archived. Compliance, forensics if needed.

Glacier (1+ years): Legal hold, compliance. Retrieve in hours/days, not minutes.

Log Management Best Practices

Log Management Implementation Guidelines

1. Collect Security-Relevant Logs First: Authentication, process execution, network connections. These detect most attacks. Add more over time.

2. Enable Command Line Logging: Process creation without command lines is nearly useless. Enable audit policy for full visibility.

3. Normalize Early: Parse and normalize at ingestion. Makes searching and correlation vastly easier downstream.

4. Time Sync Everything: NTP on all systems. Logs without accurate timestamps are useless for correlation.

5. Protect Log Integrity: Centralize immediately. Attackers delete local logs. Consider write-once storage for compliance.

6. Monitor Log Pipeline: Alert on collection failures. If logs stop flowing, you're blind—and that might be intentional.
