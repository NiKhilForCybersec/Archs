Business Continuity & Disaster Recovery

Business Continuity Planning (BCP) ensures critical business functions continue during disruptions. Disaster Recovery (DR) focuses on restoring IT systems after a disaster. Together, they minimize downtime and data loss.

Key Concepts

RTO (Recovery Time Objective)
  • Maximum acceptable downtime
  • How long can you be down?
  • Drives DR architecture
  • Example: RTO = 4 hours

Lower RTO = Higher cost (hot standby, active-active)

RPO (Recovery Point Objective)
  • Maximum acceptable data loss
  • How much data can you lose?
  • Drives backup frequency
  • Example: RPO = 1 hour

Lower RPO = Higher cost (continuous replication)

DR Tiers

Tier Strategy RTO RPO Cost
Tier 0 No DR (backup only) Days-Weeks 24+ hours $
Tier 1 Cold Site (empty facility) Days 24 hours $$
Tier 2 Warm Standby (pilot light) Hours Hours $$$
Tier 3 Hot Standby (active-passive) Minutes Minutes $$$$
Tier 4 Active-Active (multi-region) Seconds Zero $$$$$

DR Testing

Tabletop Exercise

Discussion-based walkthrough of DR plan. Low risk, low cost. Identifies gaps in procedures. Quarterly recommended.

Simulation Test

Execute DR procedures without actual failover. Test backup restoration, communication plans. Semi-annual.

Parallel Test

Bring up DR environment alongside production. Validate full functionality. Annual recommended.

Full Failover Test

Actual cutover to DR. Highest confidence but highest risk. Annual for critical systems, with rollback plan.

BCP/DRP Best Practices

BCP/DRP Implementation Guidelines

1. Start with BIA: Business Impact Analysis determines RTO/RPO for each system. Business drives requirements.

2. Test Regularly: Untested DR plans fail when needed. Annual full tests, quarterly tabletops minimum.

3. Automate Failover: Manual processes are slow and error-prone. Infrastructure as Code for DR.

4. Document Everything: Runbooks for failover/failback. Contact lists, escalation paths.

5. Consider Ransomware: Traditional DR may not help if backups are encrypted. Immutable backups, isolated recovery.

6. Review After Incidents: Every outage is a DR test. Update plans based on lessons learned.
