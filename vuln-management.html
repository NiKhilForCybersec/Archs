Vulnerability Management

Vulnerability Management is the continuous process of identifying, classifying, prioritizing, and remediating security vulnerabilities across your environment. Modern programs go beyond CVSS scores to incorporate exploit intelligence, asset criticality, and threat context for risk-based prioritization.

Vulnerability Management Lifecycle

Traditional vs. Risk-Based Vulnerability Management

Traditional: CVSS-Only
  • Prioritize by CVSS score alone
  • "Patch all criticals within 15 days"
  • Thousands of critical vulns to fix
  • No context on exploitability
  • All assets treated equally
  • Chasing numbers, not reducing risk

Problem: CVSS 9.8 vuln on isolated test server gets same priority as CVSS 7.5 vuln on internet-facing payment system with active exploit.

Risk-Based (RBVM)
  • CVSS + Exploit Intelligence + Asset Context
  • "Fix vulns being actively exploited on crown jewels"
  • Focus on ~5% of vulns that matter most
  • Real-world exploitability weighted
  • Asset criticality factored in
  • Measurable risk reduction

Result: Payment system vuln gets priority despite lower CVSS because: internet-facing + active exploit in wild + processes card data + no compensating controls.

Implementation Best Practices

Success Factors

1. Prioritize Ruthlessly: Focus on CISA KEV and actively exploited vulnerabilities first. Don't chase CVSS scores alone.

2. Know Your Assets: You can't secure what you don't know. Maintain accurate, current asset inventory with business context and ownership.

3. Automate Ticket Creation: Integrate VM platform with ITSM. Auto-create tickets with full context to reduce analyst toil.

4. Define Clear SLAs: Set risk-based remediation timelines. Critical/Exploited: 24-72 hours. High: 7-14 days. Medium: 30 days.

5. Track Mean Time to Remediate: MTTR is your key metric. If it's growing, something's broken in your process.

6. Accept Risk Formally: Not everything gets patched. Establish formal risk acceptance process for exceptions with business owner sign-off.
