Risk Management

Security risk management is the systematic process of identifying, assessing, and treating risks to organizational assets. Effective risk management enables informed decision-making, optimal resource allocation, and alignment between security investments and business objectives.

Risk Formula

Risk Assessment Process

Risk Assessment Lifecycle
1
Asset Identification
What are we protecting?
Asset Inventory
Asset Categories:
  • Data: Customer PII, financial records, IP, credentials
  • Systems: Servers, databases, applications, endpoints
  • People: Employees, contractors, customers
  • Facilities: Data centers, offices, remote locations
  • Reputation: Brand value, customer trust

Asset Valuation:
  • Business criticality (1-5)
  • Data sensitivity classification
  • Regulatory requirements
  • Revenue dependency
2
Threat Identification
Who/what could cause harm?
Threat Sources
External Threats:
  • Nation-state actors (APT groups)
  • Organized cybercrime (ransomware gangs)
  • Hacktivists
  • Competitors (corporate espionage)

Internal Threats:
  • Malicious insiders
  • Negligent employees
  • Compromised accounts

Environmental:
  • Natural disasters
  • Power failures
  • Supply chain disruption
3
Vulnerability Assessment
Where are we exposed?
Vulnerability Sources
Technical:
  • Unpatched software (CVEs)
  • Misconfigurations
  • Weak authentication
  • Insecure code

Process:
  • Lack of security training
  • No incident response plan
  • Poor change management
  • Inadequate logging

People:
  • Susceptibility to phishing
  • Weak password practices
  • Shadow IT usage
4

5
Risk Treatment
Decide how to address each risk
Treatment Options
MITIGATE: Implement controls to reduce risk
  Example: Deploy EDR to reduce malware risk
  
TRANSFER: Shift risk to third party
  Example: Cyber insurance for ransomware costs
  
ACCEPT: Acknowledge and monitor risk
  Example: Accept low-impact legacy system risk
  
AVOID: Eliminate the risk entirely
  Example: Don't store data you don't need

Risk Frameworks

NIST RMF

Risk Management Framework. 7-step process: Prepare, Categorize, Select, Implement, Assess, Authorize, Monitor. US federal standard.

ISO 31000

International risk management standard. Principles, framework, and process for managing any type of risk. Industry agnostic.

FAIR

Factor Analysis of Information Risk. Quantitative model for measuring cyber risk in financial terms. Enables ROI calculations.

OCTAVE

Operationally Critical Threat, Asset, and Vulnerability Evaluation. Self-directed risk assessment. Good for organizations new to risk management.

Risk Management Best Practices

Risk Management Implementation Guidelines

1. Executive Sponsorship: Risk management must have board/C-suite visibility. Security is a business issue, not just IT.

2. Quantify Where Possible: Use FAIR or similar to express risk in dollars. Enables comparison with business investments.

3. Continuous Process: Risk assessment is not annual checkbox. Continuous monitoring, regular reviews, event-triggered reassessment.

4. Document Everything: Risk register, treatment decisions, acceptance rationale. Creates accountability and audit trail.

5. Align to Business: Risk appetite defined by business leadership. Security implements controls to achieve risk tolerance.

6. Third-Party Risk: Vendors and partners extend your attack surface. Include supply chain in risk assessments.
