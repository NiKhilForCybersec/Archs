Email Security

Email remains the #1 attack vector for enterprises. 90%+ of cyberattacks begin with a phishing email. Email security encompasses secure email gateways (SEG), advanced threat protection, email authentication (DMARC/DKIM/SPF), and protection against business email compromise (BEC).

Email Threat Landscape

Phishing

Fraudulent emails impersonating trusted entities to steal credentials or deliver malware. Includes spear-phishing (targeted) and whaling (executives).

Business Email Compromise

Attacker impersonates CEO/CFO to trick employees into wire transfers or data disclosure. Often no malware involved—pure social engineering.

Malicious Attachments

Documents with macros, executables, or exploit code. Delivered as invoices, shipping notices, or business documents.

Credential Harvesting

Links to fake login pages (Microsoft 365, banking). Captures username/password for account takeover.

Email Security Architecture

Email Authentication: SPF, DKIM, DMARC

Protocol What It Does Protects Against
SPF Lists authorized sending IPs for domain in DNS TXT record Direct domain spoofing from unauthorized servers
DKIM Cryptographically signs emails; receiver verifies signature Message tampering, proves sender authenticity
DMARC Policy for handling SPF/DKIM failures; enables reporting Tells receivers what to do with failed emails (reject/quarantine)
DNS Records Example
# SPF Record - Who can send email for your domain
contoso.com.  TXT  "v=spf1 include:spf.protection.outlook.com 
                    include:_spf.google.com -all"

# DKIM Record - Public key for signature verification
selector1._domainkey.contoso.com.  TXT  "v=DKIM1; k=rsa; p=MIIBIj..."

# DMARC Record - Policy + reporting
_dmarc.contoso.com.  TXT  "v=DMARC1; p=reject; rua=mailto:[email protected]; 
                           ruf=mailto:[email protected]; pct=100"

# DMARC Policy Levels:
#   p=none      → Monitor only, don't take action (start here)
#   p=quarantine → Send failures to spam/junk
#   p=reject    → Block failures entirely (goal state)

Email Security Best Practices

Email Security Implementation Guidelines

1. Implement DMARC at Reject: Start with p=none to monitor, move to quarantine, then reject. Protects your domain from spoofing.

2. Layer Email Security: SEG + native platform protection (Defender ATP). Defense in depth catches what one misses.

3. Enable Safe Links/Attachments: URL rewriting with time-of-click scanning. Sandbox attachments before delivery.

4. External Email Banners: Clear warning on emails from outside organization. Helps users spot impersonation.

5. Phishing Simulations: Regular simulated phishing campaigns. Train users, measure improvement, target training.

6. Report Phishing Button: Make it easy for users to report suspicious emails. Enables rapid response and learning.
