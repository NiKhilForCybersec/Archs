Insider Threat

Insider threats originate from individuals with legitimate access—employees, contractors, or partners—who misuse that access intentionally or inadvertently. Detecting and preventing insider threats requires a combination of technical controls, behavioral analytics, and organizational measures.

Insider Threat Types

Malicious Insider

Intentional harm—data theft, sabotage, fraud. Often motivated by financial gain, revenge, or ideology. Hardest to detect.

Negligent Insider

Unintentional harm through carelessness—clicking phishing links, misconfiguring systems, losing devices. Most common type.

Compromised Insider

Legitimate user whose credentials are stolen. Attacker operates using valid credentials. Often indistinguishable initially.

Third-Party Insider

Contractor, vendor, or partner with access. May have different motivations or weaker security controls.

Behavioral Indicators

Insider Threat Indicators
# Technical Indicators (Detectable)

DATA EXFILTRATION PATTERNS:
• Large data downloads unusual for role
• USB usage spikes, especially before departure
• Email to personal accounts with attachments
• Cloud storage uploads (Dropbox, Google Drive)
• After-hours access to sensitive repositories

ACCESS ANOMALIES:
• Access to systems outside job function
• Privilege escalation attempts
• Accessing departing colleague's files
• VPN from unusual locations
• Failed access to restricted resources

BEHAVIORAL ANOMALIES:
• Login time changes (suddenly working late)
• Increased printing activity
• Bypassing security controls
• Screenshot/screen recording tools
• Disabling endpoint security

# HR/Organizational Indicators (Context)

HIGH-RISK SITUATIONS:
• Recently passed over for promotion
• Notice given (departing employee)
• Under investigation or PIP
• Financial difficulties (bankruptcy, divorce)
• Ideological conflicts expressed
• Working with competitor recruiter

BEHAVIORAL CHANGES:
• Sudden interest in other departments
• Asking about projects outside scope
• Working odd hours without explanation
• Hostility toward organization

Insider Threat Best Practices

Insider Threat Program Guidelines

1. Cross-Functional Team: Security, HR, Legal, Management must collaborate. No single team owns insider risk.

2. Baseline Normal Behavior: UEBA needs baseline. Know what normal looks like before detecting anomalies.

3. Departing Employee Process: Enhanced monitoring for employees who give notice. Immediate access revocation on termination.

4. Privacy Balance: Monitor proportionally. Legal review of monitoring policies. Employee transparency where possible.

5. Least Privilege: Reduce insider risk by reducing access. Users can't steal what they can't access.

6. Reporting Culture: Enable anonymous reporting. Employees often notice behavioral changes first.
